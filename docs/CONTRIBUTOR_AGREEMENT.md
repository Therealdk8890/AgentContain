# Contributor Agreement Policy — Draft

> **DRAFT — NOT YET IN FORCE.**
>
> This document is a planning artifact for establishing WarrantKit's contributor IP process. It is not a signed agreement, does not create a contract by itself, and should not be presented to contributors for signature until reviewed and approved by qualified legal counsel and the project's legal owner.

## Intended policy

WarrantKit intends to remain Apache-2.0 licensed while maintaining clear provenance for material third-party contributions.

Before accepting material contributions from outside the project owner, WarrantKit intends to use a contributor license agreement (CLA) appropriate to the contributor's circumstances.

The final agreement should address, at minimum:

- the contributor's authority to make the contribution;
- copyright licensing for present and future contributions;
- patent licensing for relevant contributions;
- third-party material and license disclosure;
- employer or corporate ownership issues;
- treatment of contributions made on behalf of an organization;
- preservation of the contributor's rights outside WarrantKit;
- records and retention of signed agreements;
- the relationship between the CLA and the Apache License 2.0.

## Intended operating model

### Individual contributors

A material contribution from an individual should be accepted only after the project has a signed individual CLA on file, unless the maintainer has determined that the contribution falls within an explicitly documented exception.

### Corporate contributors

Where an employer or other legal entity owns or may own the contribution, the project should obtain the appropriate corporate authorization/CLA before accepting the material contribution.

An individual contributor should not represent that they can license work to WarrantKit when their employer or another entity may own the relevant rights.

### Small contributions

Routine documentation fixes, typo corrections, or similarly low-risk contributions may be handled under the project's normal Apache-2.0 contribution terms if the final contribution policy permits that exception.

The project should not use the "small contribution" exception to bypass provenance review for security-critical, architectural, cryptographic, or otherwise material changes.

## Recordkeeping

Signed agreements should be retained in a controlled location separate from the public source repository unless counsel determines that publication is appropriate.

The repository should retain enough public provenance information to connect material contributions to the applicable contributor record without exposing unnecessary personal information.

## Open-source license

A CLA is not intended to change WarrantKit's Apache-2.0 license.

The purpose is to clarify the project's rights to accept, maintain, modify, distribute, and continue licensing contributed material and to reduce ambiguity during security, investment, partnership, or acquisition diligence.

## Before activation

Before this policy is enforced:

1. Identify the legal owner of WarrantKit's intellectual property.
2. Have counsel select or draft the final individual and corporate contributor agreements.
3. Define the exact copyright and patent grants.
4. Define whether the project owner receives a right to sublicense, transfer, or otherwise continue the project if the business is sold or reorganized.
5. Establish a secure process for collecting and retaining signed agreements.
6. Decide which contribution classes, if any, are exempt from a signed CLA.
7. Document how the policy will be enforced for pull requests and commit access.

Until those steps are complete, this file is informational only.

## Why this exists

The goal is not to make WarrantKit less open.

The goal is to keep the Apache-2.0 open core genuinely open while maintaining a clean chain of rights for material contributions. That distinction becomes increasingly important as the project gains outside contributors, commercial users, and potential strategic partners or acquirers.
